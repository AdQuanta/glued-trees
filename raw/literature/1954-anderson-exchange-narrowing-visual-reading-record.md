The paper constructs a stochastic line-shape model for resonances whose local frequency changes through exchange or motion. It treats the local environments as a set of possible resonance frequencies connected by stochastic transitions, formulates the response through the corresponding transition matrix, and studies the poles that determine the observed spectrum.

In the slow-exchange regime, separate components remain associated with the local environments. As exchange becomes faster, those components move together and ultimately merge. In the rapid-exchange regime, fluctuations are averaged and the surviving line is narrowed: its residual width is controlled by the frequency-spread variance divided by the exchange or motion rate. This is the mathematical origin of the motional- or exchange-narrowing analogy used in the project wiki.

The result concerns temporal averaging produced by stochastic exchange. It is therefore an analogy for disorder suppression, not direct evidence that a static Hamiltonian with sparse intra-layer edges protects the glued-tree transport manifold.
