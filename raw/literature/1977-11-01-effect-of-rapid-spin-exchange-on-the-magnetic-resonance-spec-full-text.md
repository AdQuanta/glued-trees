                 It was recently discovered that when there are sufficiently rapid spin-exchange collisions in an alkali-metal
               vapor, the rf magnetic-resonance    frequency of the ground-state atoms becomes a constant fractional value
               (e.g. , 4/11 for Cs) of the Larmor frequency, and the linewidth becomes inversely proportional to the spin-
               exchange rate. These properties are in complete contrast to those in the slow spin-exchange limit. We
               present a theoretical analysis of these properties, and we predict some additional new effects for rapid spin
               exchange. Four theories are presented: an intuitive-vector model, a perturbation treatment, numerical
               solutions of complex non-Hermitian       matrices, and a statistical analysis of the effect of rapid transfers
               between two Zeeman multiplets. These theories all provide, to varying degrees, consistent quantitative
               answers to the problem of how spin exchange affects magnetic resonance in an alkali vapor.
