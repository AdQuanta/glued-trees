## Role in the paper

This literature imposes microscopic discipline on phrases such as collective mode, linewidth, shift, and protection. It is particularly useful for distinguishing a coherent spectral reorganization from a merely phenomenological decay fit.

## Linear response and causality

For a weakly driven two-level transition, the polarizability has resonant and counter-rotating contributions of the form

$$
\alpha(\omega)\propto
\frac{1}{\omega_0-\omega-i\gamma/2}
+\frac{1}{\omega_0+\omega+i\gamma/2}.
$$

The signs and linewidth assignments are fixed by causality and the optical theorem, not by a freely chosen phenomenological denominator. Retarded two-atom calculations further show that exchange contains light-travel delays and repeated propagation; instantaneous couplings are an approximation with a stated regime of validity.

## Collective modes and disorder

Superradiant and subradiant modes arise from interference among emitters. Position correlations, near-field interactions, and inhomogeneous shifts can either sharpen or wash out the collective response. This is conceptually close to a symmetric glued-tree sector coupled to many nonsymmetric modes, but the open radiative problem is not the project Hamiltonian.
