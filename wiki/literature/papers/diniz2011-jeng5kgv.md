## Scope and technical content

Shows that strong collective cavity coupling can spectrally separate polaritons from an inhomogeneous dark continuum and thereby narrow their decay. Protection improves only when the emitter distribution falls faster than a Lorentzian in the tails; it is therefore a self-energy/spectral-density result, not a universal consequence of a large Rabi splitting.

## Main derivation or method

Shows that strong collective cavity coupling can spectrally separate polaritons from an inhomogeneous dark continuum and thereby narrow their decay.

## Main result

Protection improves only when the emitter distribution falls faster than a Lorentzian in the tails; it is therefore a self-energy/spectral-density result, not a universal consequence of a large Rabi splitting.

## Mathematical core

In the one-excitation sector, the cavity–ensemble model is

$$
H=\omega_c a^\dagger a+\sum_j\omega_j\sigma_j^+\sigma_j^-
+\sum_jg_j(a^\dagger\sigma_j^-+a\sigma_j^+).
$$

The bright collective coupling is $\Omega=(\sum_j|g_j|^2)^{1/2}$. Integrating out the inhomogeneous ensemble produces a frequency-dependent cavity self-energy. The polariton linewidth samples the ensemble spectral density near the split polariton energies, which is why tail shape—not only width—controls cavity protection.
